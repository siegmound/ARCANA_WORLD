## Atomic eligibility and recovery

RecoveryController is an opt-in synthetic adapter over the unchanged B0 store.
Its `start`, `advance`, `commit`, `recover`, `history` and `close` methods do not
replace the original runner. Publication order:

1. Finish the compact chunk, flush/fsync it and durably install its immutable
   content-addressed link in this run's directory.
2. Seal and verify the full coupled native checkpoint, fsync its bytes and
   immutable directory entry. Replay compact deltas in one private buffer;
   compare regional cells, thermal state digests, total/viscous strain and Deep
   inventory against the checkpoint, and verify annual balances/clocks.
3. Use one B0 `append_transaction` to publish a typed FIXTURE_ONLY descriptor
   and CheckpointEnvelope under the expected parent read-view. B0's existing
   CURRENT read-view switch is the sole visibility point.
4. Clean staged links and open the next chunk only after successful publication.

The descriptor binds source/config/plan, native digest, chunk byte length/SHA,
local chain and first/last local/global sequences, count by kind, both clocks,
and parent B0 checkpoint. The B0 envelope binds descriptor identity/content and
the current SHA set of all R6 Python modules. A restored runtime starts its own
local chain; the parent descriptor and global ordinals preserve causal continuity.
No native checkpoint schema/hash was changed. Its historical NOT_IMPLEMENTED
chain placeholder remains a solver-only field; REC1 resume authority is the
verified committed B0 descriptor, never that placeholder or a loose checkpoint.

Recovery selects the unique committed lineage and verifies every chunk/native
pair before resuming. Orphan active/chunk/frontier files are ineligible and retained
for diagnosis. A corrupt committed pair raises an error; no silent rollback
or blind retry. A failed live controller requires recovery. Old readers retain
their pinned B0 view; a stale writer fails CAS. Public reconstructed snapshots
are detached, including year zero.

## Limits and next gate

Process-crash consistency is qualified on the current FAIR filesystem and B0
fsync/rename model. Hardware power-loss, storage-server failover and malicious
concurrent file modification were not tested. Content hashes provide integrity,
not authentication against an actor able to replace the complete store.

The five O(D) numerical scans and resident R2 encoded entries/frames remain.
REC1 adds duplicate encoding to the durable writer; no constant-memory claim.
Commit costs O(D + new compact chunk) and reconstructs a single frontier buffer,
without full historical snapshot copies per year. Recovery verifies all committed
chunks and currently retains native checkpoints for the selected chain:
O(total compact history + sum of checkpoint sizes) work/storage in memory,
plus replay buffers. Cold full-history queries repeat validation then build
requested full snapshots; full materialization remains quadratic output work.
Long-run integration must expose these costs rather than bury them in the writer.

The draft 1000-year checkpoint interval after Y1000 implies up to 999 years
recomputation; it remains a proposal. Short overhead does not establish optimal
cadence. Evaluate more frequent commits against measured later-state checkpoint
costs and the required recovery objective. No adaptive scientific retention or
pruning is introduced.
