# B6N8-B — Targeted scientific research for a reduced-order evolvable rift model

## Decision and scope

Qualified source baseline: `e2d9d3e4ffc70bb665d1b150e1a2e34fae6fbc42` on
`r6/b6n8b-targeted-rift-model-research`. This is a literature screening, not
a model selection or implementation. B6N7 remains the governing requirement
set; B6N8-A remains a numerical/geometric diagnostic only.

**Finding:** the literature contains plausible reduced or parameterized
process-model families, but this review did not identify an off-the-shelf
Tier-2 model that, by itself, supplies all B6N7 outputs on ARCANA's present
synthetic interface: process-state evolution, support-resolved interface
response, conservative transition predicates, topology-event coverage and
support-membership validity. The strongest Tier-2 research candidate is a
Buck-style reduced thin-sheet/force-and-thermal-state model. It is a candidate
for comparative prototyping, not a selected or validated ARCANA model. It
predicts a model-specific widening/localization response under its own
assumptions, but that response is not a plate split, topology result or
general support-remapping rule.

The evidence therefore supports **`PASS_B6N8B_TIER2_MODEL_FAMILIES_IDENTIFIED`**
and **`READY_FOR_TARGETED_PROTOTYPE_COMPARISON`**, with a material caveat:
prototype comparison must first establish whether the candidate state and
inputs can be authored for a synthetic world and must treat interface,
topology and support predicates as separate outputs requiring separate
coverage. No claim is made that Tier 2 already closes B6N4-R1. Full mechanics
is not proven necessary; neither is its irrelevance proven.

## What the ARCANA baseline actually supplies

B6N7 governs an active process identity for plate pair 1:3, the same-age
PRE_EVENT/POST_EVENT boundary, the preserved 71-segment interface and
junction/support identities, and restricted conditional rigid Euler
kinematics. It does not classify segments, establish extension/accommodation,
or authorize topology change. B6N8-A integrated that kinematic forcing as a
shadow diagnostic and found small cross-resolution numerical differences;
its side-to-side geometric mismatch was explicitly not interpreted as
opening, strain or physical interface response. Additional temporal
refinement did not close the physical-model, transition or support gaps.

Consequently, pairwise rigid motion is at most a candidate forcing after
interface frame/sign and validity are governed. It cannot initialize a
physical extension state by itself. B6N8-A's 72 nodes and 71 segment identities
are usable as diagnostic support references, not as 71 already classified
faults. The experiment's 2053.512977-year window and RK4 step sizes are not a
physical interval, `dt2`, or a transition horizon.

## Family findings

### Tier 1: kinematic extension and prescribed fault histories

Kinematic models can propagate geometry from supplied fault geometry,
displacements and their time histories. RIFTER is an example: its published
description calls it a kinematic forward deformation model, with fault
geometry, displacement and distributed pure-shear deformation supplied as
functions of time. That is useful for structural reconstruction and scenario
comparison, but the extension/fault history is input, not an emergent
process-state output. It cannot provide ARCANA's missing next-event predicate
unless an externally governed history or threshold is added. It is rejected
as a standalone B6N5 blocker solution.

Pure relative-motion integration has the same boundary: it yields a geometric
diagnostic. A physical normal extension rate would first require an authorized
interface normal, polarity/sign, support mapping and constitutive meaning.
Even then, integrating it only gives a chosen kinematic measure; a physical
response law and transition criteria remain additional assumptions.

### Tier 3: stretching-factor and basin thermal/subsidence models

The McKenzie uniform-stretching model relates an imposed finite stretching
factor to crust/lithosphere thinning and subsequent thermal subsidence/heat
flow. The original construction treats stretching as a rapid event followed
by conductive thermal relaxation. The stretching factor is specified or
inferred from observations; it is not generated by a runtime rift process
law. Finite-rate and depth-dependent descendants represent more temporal or
vertical structure, but many remain forward response models conditional on a
prescribed extension history. These are valuable downstream physical
response models if extension is already authorized; they do not independently
decide whether ARCANA's active interface begins, localizes, migrates, fails,
or changes topology.

Crustal thinning, stretching ratio, temperature, heat flow and subsidence are
not universal minimum state variables. They become required only for a
selected law that uses or claims them. Their initialization and material,
thermal and boundary parameters are absent from the present B6N8-A forcing
diagnostic and cannot be inferred from its geometric mismatch.

### Tier 2 candidate: reduced thin-sheet/force and thermal-state models

Buck (1991) is the most relevant reduced candidate found. It combines a thin
sheet estimate of lithospheric yield strength and buoyancy with thermal
advection/diffusion and lower-crustal flow. It evaluates how required
extension force changes after finite extension; the model assumes widening
when that force increases and localization when it decreases. Thus, unlike a
pure prescribed-geometry model, it has evolving physical quantities and a
model-internal mode response that can be evaluated as the run proceeds.

That makes the family worth testing, but it is not a generic scalar ODE
drop-in. Its response depends on crustal thickness, thermal state, heat
production, density contrast, brittle strength gradient, temperature/rate
dependent ductile strength, lower-crust flow and applied strain rate. Several
of these are specialist physical inputs not currently governed for the active
synthetic pair. Its widening/localization criterion is a model-specific
mechanical/force-balance decision. It does not label ARCANA segments, resolve
junction compatibility, guarantee topology-event coverage, or define how
support membership changes. The paper's mode output can at most nominate a
scope/regime reevaluation; a separate ARCANA transition contract must decide
what topology/support operation is eligible.

**Reduction limit:** do not silently remove thermal, strength, thickness or
flow variables if they control the force trend that triggers the published
mode response. A lower-dimensional surrogate could be studied only after
showing which causal dependencies it preserves across the intended parameter
domain. No such reduction is performed here.

### Tier 4 reference: thermo-mechanical continuum and fault-network evolution

Thermo-mechanical models with visco-plastic rheology, strain/friction
weakening, thermal evolution, faults/shear zones and boundary forcing can
produce evolving localization, fault interaction, thinning and rift migration
as model outputs. Published studies show that these outcomes depend on
coupled rheology, inherited heterogeneity, initial thermal/material structure
and imposed extension. They are the strongest references for emergent
interface response and localization, but their state is a spatial mechanical
field, not a small, already validated ARCANA vector. The required equations,
constitutive choices and fields make these Tier-4 references rather than
evidence that full mechanics is necessary for the minimum B6N7 predicates.

Fault-controlled network models are not a free reduction: if faults are
explicitly created, propagated, linked or abandoned, the model needs a
governed nucleation/weakness representation, fault interaction law, stress or
equivalent driving state, and a mapping from fault segments to ARCANA support.
If a paper prescribes fault locations or slip history, it is a kinematic input
scenario, not an emergent fault-transition law. No fault network or polarity
is imported from Earth into ARCANA.

## Prescribed history versus model output

| Family | Extension/history role | What may emerge | B6N8-B disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rigid/kinematic extension | Relative plate motion is supplied; segment-normal physical extension is not yet governed | Coordinate/displacement diagnostic | Reject alone; no process or response law |
| McKenzie/stretching-factor basin model | Stretching factor / extension episode is prescribed or inferred before response calculation | Thermal relaxation, subsidence, heat flow after stretching | Downstream Tier 3 response; not the process initiator |
| Finite-rate/depth-dependent thermal stretching | Extension-rate history is generally input | Temperature/thinning/subsidence response | Conditional Tier 3; predicates still need authority |
| Buck reduced force/thermal mode model | Applied extension/strain rate and physical initial/material state are inputs | Force trend and assumed widening/localization mode | Shortlisted Tier-2 candidate, conditional and model-specific |
| RIFTER-like structural kinematics | Fault geometry/displacement histories are input | Cross-section geometry and isostatic response | Reject as blocker closure; valuable scenario/reconstruction tool |
| Thermo-mechanical continuum | Boundary forcing is prescribed; deformation/faulting is solved from constitutive equations and initial structure | Localization, faulting, thinning and regime evolution | Tier-4 reference; high model/data burden |
| Prescribed event/reconstruction chronology | Event ages or geological history are supplied | Interpolated/reconstructed states | Excluded: fails synthetic predictive-process requirement |

The distinction matters for 210 Ma → 0 ka: an input trajectory can replay a
chosen scenario but cannot forecast an unprovided synthetic world's transition
without a model generating the transition signal.

## Candidate state and forcing

No state vector is frozen. A Buck-like candidate would need, at minimum, a
state sufficient to reproduce the model's evolving extension, thermal/strength
response and mode criterion. Depending on the exact formulation, this can
include spatially resolved crust/lithosphere thickness, temperature or thermal
proxy, extension/strain, lower-crust redistribution/flow and strength/force
diagnostics. These are candidate requirements of that family, not selected
ARCANA fields. A simplified accumulator such as `cumulative_extension` is
insufficient unless it is shown to preserve the mechanisms and predicates.

Candidate forcing is restricted B6N2 relative rigid motion, but using it as
extension requires a model-owned mapping from spherical plate motion to local
interface coordinates and explicit sign/polarity and support. A reduced
physical model may also need far-field force/traction or imposed extension
rate, initial lithospheric geometry, thermal state, composition/material
properties, inherited weaknesses, radiogenic heating, surface/sediment loads,
and possibly magma/mantle inputs. None is presumed available. Do not infer
these from Earth analogues or substitute B6N8-A mismatch.

## Runtime predicates and B6N4-R1 blockers

Published model regimes may provide a process-validity or localization signal
only where the source model defines an actual state-dependent diagnostic or
criterion. A crossing is not an event application.

- **NEXT_RIFT_PROCESS_EVOLUTION:** a reduced model with explicit state and
  evolution law can address it directly within declared validity. Static
  beta/stretching models and prescribed histories cannot.
- **PLATE_INTERFACE_EVENT_PREDICATES:** model response must map to the 71
  identified segments or a rigorously declared aggregate. Existing candidate
  models mostly evolve 2-D cross-sections or regional fields; no automatic
  segment mapping or uniform response is defensible.
- **GENERIC_TOPOLOGY_EVENT_COVERAGE:** localization, necking, or breakup can
  signal topology reevaluation, but cannot determine a plate split, new
  boundary graph, junction rewiring, or event coverage across all required
  classes. Separate topology model/application authority remains necessary.
- **SUPPORT_MEMBERSHIP_VALIDITY:** none of the reviewed families establishes
  ARCANA's set-valued support membership/remapping contract. At most a model
  domain or active-width estimate can request reevaluation; it cannot silently
  rewrite membership.

The full cell-by-cell family coverage is in the JSON matrix. Missing coverage
is `UNKNOWN`/`NOT_ADDRESSED`, not inferred from a model's ability to output
stress, strain, a fault map, or a rift stage label.

## Adaptive integration, events, locality and replay

State-bearing ODE/finite-volume/reduced models can in principle be advanced
with varying `INTERNAL_DT` if their equations, forcing interpolation,
stability/error estimators and state transitions support variable steps. That
compatibility must be shown for the selected equations; B6N6's architecture
does not itself establish model stability. Predicate event bracketing requires
a defined continuous (or explicitly discrete) predicate, valid detector
coverage, quantified uncertainty and stop behavior. A published qualitative
phase label is not automatically a bracketable predicate.

Local interface execution is plausible only when causal dependencies are
closed: thin-sheet or thermal diffusion can couple a broader cross-section;
stress redistribution and faults can couple adjacent segments; mantle and
surface-process feedback may enlarge the domain. A local 71-segment strip is
not assumed independent. Demonstrate buffer convergence or conservatively
expand scope before claiming local validity.

Replay requires model/code version, parameter authority, initial state,
forcing sequence, support and dependency closure, solver and accepted/rejected
steps, uncertainty settings, predicate versions and any stochastic seed. A
deterministic solver may still have numerical/platform variation; preserve
that as numerical uncertainty instead of claiming bitwise equivalence without
evidence. Parameter and model-form uncertainty should be propagated as
separate dimensions or retained as `UNKNOWN`; Earth calibration ranges are not
automatically valid for a synthetic planet.

## Computational and software assessment

A compact reduced model per active rift scope is computationally plausible
for repeated long histories, with local refinement at supported predicate
approaches. This is an algorithmic plausibility assessment, not a measured
210 Ma runtime estimate. Thermo-mechanical continuum experiments are commonly
2-D/3-D mesh-based and solve coupled fields repeatedly; using them globally at
high resolution is a substantial HPC undertaking. A practical architecture
could screen globally with a reduced process model and call an explicitly
qualified local high-fidelity model only when justified, but that hybrid
surrogate requires validation and cannot be assumed equivalent.

No software is selected or reused. ASPECT is an open-source computational
geodynamics code under GPL v2 or later; Underworld provides particle-in-cell
finite-element Stokes-flow geodynamics and exposes user-defined model physics.
They are Tier-4 implementation references, not prepackaged rift laws. Any code
reuse would require a separate dependency, version, build/runtime and license
compatibility review. RIFTER is a model-family reference; no license or
redistribution grant was established by this literature screen.

## Shortlist and adversarial assessment

### Candidate 1 — Buck-style reduced thin-sheet force/mode evolution

**Why shortlist:** an explicit evolving physical calculation and a
state-dependent widening/localization response exists in a reduced
approximation; it is more than imposed rigid motion or post-rift subsidence.

**Why it may fail:** it embeds force-balance assumptions and material/thermal
inputs; its mode switch is a specific model assumption, not a universal rift
predicate. It has no ARCANA junction, per-segment interface, general topology,
or support-remapping semantics. Synthetic-world transferability and parameter
identifiability are open. It may already be mechanics-dependent in the sense
relevant to B6N7; the research did not establish a mechanically independent
reduction.

**Next evidence:** recover the full governing equations and original
assumptions; independently trace every mode criterion; test whether a small
state reduction preserves those criteria under varied initial/material
conditions; define which inputs are authorial synthetic primitives versus
literature-constrained parameters. No coefficient values are selected here.

### Candidate 2 — reduced fault-network evolution

**Why shortlist conditionally:** segment state/activity and interaction could
align naturally with interface predicates and support-scoped events.

**Why it may fail:** published fault evolution often relies on mechanical
stress/strain fields or prescribed inherited faults; a network growth law
without a governed stress/weakness/nucleation state is descriptive or
arbitrary. Junction compatibility and global event coverage remain difficult.
This is a research direction, not yet an identified sufficient published
Tier-2 model.

### Tier-4 reference set — thermo-mechanical localization models

Use Huismans & Beaumont, Brune et al., and later rift simulations as
mechanistic benchmarks for which state feedbacks and regime diagnostics a
reduced model must preserve. These are not recommended as immediate production
providers. They have strong hidden input dependence (rheology, temperature,
weakness, initial structure, forcing) and much larger computational and
integration burden.

No other reviewed family merits a standalone shortlist: prescribed-history
basin models, simple stretching-factor models, plate kinematics, and
kinematic fault reconstructions fail the predictive-process exclusion test.

## Lowest sufficient tier and next experiment

B6N7's **Tier 2 remains the lowest requirement tier to investigate**, not a
finding that Tier 2 is already sufficient. Evidence suggests a state-bearing
reduced physical model may provide process/regime evolution, but a candidate
will need additional explicitly governed interface-response mapping and
separate topology/support validity predicates. Tier 3 becomes necessary only
if the selected claims require crust/lithosphere thickness or thermal state;
Tier 4 becomes necessary only if the selected transition criterion cannot be
supported without force balance, stress/strain, fracture, or mechanical
accommodation. The available literature does not adjudicate that necessity.

Recommended next action: a **targeted prototype-comparison design**, not
implementation. Compare (1) the reduced Buck-style process-state/force-mode
candidate and (2) a fault-network process candidate, using the same synthetic
interface and a requirements-derived observable checklist. First obtain
author decisions on whether T2's constitutive inputs may be authored as
synthetic initial conditions and whether the target transition is regime
reevaluation only or includes crustal/topology claims. Then obtain source-level
equations, uncertainty ranges, and criterion provenance. Keep all four B6N4-R1
blockers independently visible. If neither candidate can make defensible
interface and conservative invalidation predicates without ungoverned inputs,
escalate the question to Tier 3/4 rather than tuning a proxy.

## Unresolved evidence gaps

1. Full equation-level re-derivation and implementation-level audit of the
   Buck model's state, force trend and widening rule.
2. Whether its parameterization can be initialized from an ARCANA synthetic
   world without importing Earth-specific calibration.
3. A defensible mapping from model response support to ARCANA's existing
   segment and junction identities.
4. Conservative coverage of plate split/merge/creation, boundary birth/death,
   adjacency change, junction rewiring and support invalidation.
5. Which finite physical state is genuinely needed for the chosen event claim,
   versus merely useful for downstream outputs.
6. Validity/error bounds under variable `INTERNAL_DT`, event bracketing and
   temporal/spatial local refinement.
7. Parameter, forcing, model-form, localization and event-time uncertainty
   for synthetic-world use.
8. Scope/dependency closure for local operation and runtime cost over a
   210 Ma history.
9. Software version, license compatibility and reproducible build for any
   future code candidate.

## Scientific provenance

Each source below is typed as a primary model/paper, review, or software
implementation/documentation. Claims above are restricted to the model
formulation, assumptions or results represented by that source; transfer to
ARCANA is an assessment/inference and is labeled as such.

1. **PRIMARY —** McKenzie, D. (1978), “Some remarks on the development of
   sedimentary basins,” *Earth and Planetary Science Letters* 40, 25–32,
   [doi:10.1016/0012-821X(78)90071-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-821X(78)90071-7).
   Uniform lithospheric stretching, thinning and post-stretch thermal
   evolution; supports the prescribed stretching versus subsequent response
   distinction.
2. **PRIMARY —** Buck, W. R. (1991), “Modes of continental lithospheric
   extension,” *Journal of Geophysical Research* 96(B12), 20161–20178,
   [doi:10.1029/91JB01485](https://doi.org/10.1029/91JB01485). Thin-sheet
   strength/buoyancy, thermal advection/diffusion, lower-crust flow and the
   model's force-change widening/localization rule.
3. **PRIMARY —** Huismans, R. S. & Beaumont, C. (2003), “Symmetric and
   asymmetric lithospheric extension: Relative effects of frictional-plastic
   and viscous strain softening,” *JGR: Solid Earth* 108(B10), 2496,
   [doi:10.1029/2002JB002026](https://doi.org/10.1029/2002JB002026). Plane-strain
   thermo-mechanical finite-element experiments; extension velocity, rheology
   and strain softening control modes/asymmetry.
4. **PRIMARY —** Brune, S., Heine, C., Perez-Gussinye, M. & Sobolev, S. V.
   (2014), “Rift migration explains continental margin asymmetry and crustal
   hyper-extension,” *Nature Communications* 5, 4014,
   [doi:10.1038/ncomms5014](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms5014). Coupled
   thermo-mechanical model with prescribed boundary extension and
   parameterized frictional/viscous weakening; emergent faulting/migration is
   conditional on those choices.
5. **PRIMARY —** Huismans, R. S. & Beaumont, C. (2011), “Depth-dependent
   extension, two-stage breakup and cratonic underplating at rifted margins,”
   *Nature* 473, 74–78,
   [doi:10.1038/nature09988](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09988). Numerical
   thermo-mechanical evolution under imposed extension; an upper-tier
   comparison for evolving margin states.
6. **PRIMARY —** Chenin, P. et al. (2018), “Necking of the Lithosphere: A
   Reappraisal of Basic Concepts With Thermo-Mechanical Numerical Modeling,”
   *JGR: Solid Earth*, [doi:10.1029/2017JB014155](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017JB014155).
   Connects necking to amplification of perturbations relative to bulk
   extension and compares analytical versus thermo-mechanical descriptions.
7. **PRIMARY —** White, N. & Bellingham, P. (2002), “A two-dimensional
   inverse model for extensional sedimentary basins. 1. Theory,” *JGR: Solid
   Earth* 107(B10), 2259,
   [doi:10.1029/2001JB000173](https://doi.org/10.1029/2001JB000173). The
   inverse method allows strain rate to vary through space and time without
   requiring the number, duration or intensity of rifting episodes to be
   specified a priori. The paper discusses limits of commonly used
   instantaneous-stretching formulations for representing syn-rift temporal
   evolution; this does not mean White & Bellingham's inverse method assumes
   instantaneous stretching. For ARCANA, its relevance is the distinction
   between an inferred/prescribed strain-rate history and forward process
   dynamics that generate rift evolution.
8. **PRIMARY —** Jarvis, G. T. & McKenzie, D. P. (1980), “Sedimentary basin
   formation with finite extension rates,” *Earth and Planetary Science
   Letters* 48, 42–52,
   [doi:10.1016/0012-821X(80)90168-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-821X(80)90168-5).
   Its time-dependent analysis compares finite-rate extension with the simpler
   instantaneous-stretching model and states constraints on that

9. **PRIMARY / SOFTWARE MODEL DESCRIPTION —** Toth, J. et al. (1996),

11. **REVIEW —** “Geodynamics of continental rift initiation and evolution,”
